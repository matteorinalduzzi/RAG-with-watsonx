# Enanching RAG systems with HAP language filtering

The integration of a Hate, Abuse, and Profane (HAP) language filters within Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems is critical for ensuring the ethical and responsible use of large language models (LLMs). As RAG systems combine retrieval-based search with the generative power of LLMs, they are often exposed to vast quantities of content, including documents that may contain harmful language, as well to users' queries that can push the LLM to produce HAP content. Implementing an effective HAP filter protects users from exposure to offensive content, mitigates legal and ethical risks, and fosters a safer environment for knowledge dissemination.

When it comes to filtering HAP content in the context of a RAG system, we can identify two primary approaches: filtering the ingested documents before they are stored in the system, or filtering the output generated by the LLM. Each approach presents distinct benefits and challenges. Filtering documents during ingestion ensures that harmful content is never introduced into the knowledge base, preventing the possibility of such content being retrieved or surfaced in the model’s output. On the other hand, filtering the output of the LLM can be useful in cases where harmful language is inadvertently generated or inferred based on user queries, but this approach does not preclude the system from retrieving or working with problematic content behind the scenes.

Furthermore, a major limitation of most current HAP filtering approaches is that they primarily focus on removing HAP language entirely: while this approach is effective in sanitizing content, it can also result in the loss of important information, as certain documents may contain valuable insights or critical context that happens to be conveyed in HAP language. Removing such content outright can indeed lead to incomplete or biased knowledge, reducing the overall value provided by the RAG system.

To address this, we will show how to easily implement a more nuanced pipeline that identifies HAP language within documents intended for ingestion and, rather than discarding these sections, pass the flagged content to an LLM, which will rephrase the HAP elements into a more appropriate form, preserving the original meaning and context. The cleaned documents can then be ingested into the RAG system, ensuring both the integrity of the knowledge base and the ethical use of language. This approach not only safeguards the user experience but also maximizes the amount of usable, meaningful content available to the system.
